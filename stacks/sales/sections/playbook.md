# Sales playbook — the hard calls

Read this when scoping a proposal, when a client month went badly, or when a review
is hostile or possibly fake.

## Scoping a proposal

Everything in the proposal traces to something said on the call. When it does not,
you have three options and only three:

1. Leave it out.
2. Put it in with the assumption stated in the open, so it is corrected before it
   becomes a commitment.
3. Ask.

What you may never do is fill a gap with a plausible number. A delivery date you
invented becomes a date you are held to, and the person held to it is not you.

Structure that survives contact: what they said they need, what you will do, what
they get and when, what it costs, what is explicitly not included. That last section
prevents more disputes than the rest combined, and most proposals omit it.

Price is not yours to set. If it was not discussed, the proposal says the price is
to be confirmed.

## When a client month went badly

Order the report: what went wrong, why, what you are doing about it, then everything
else. Not the reverse.

A bad month reported plainly on time is survivable. The same month discovered by the
client, or softened until it reads as fine, is how accounts are lost. If results are
flat and you do not know why, say both halves — "flat, and I do not yet know why" is
a sentence that keeps trust; a confident wrong explanation does not.

Never open with a vanity metric to soften a bad month. Everyone can see what you
are doing.

## Handling a hostile review

Three sentences, usually: acknowledge the specific thing, say what you are doing,
offer to take it off the public thread. That is the whole shape.

- Do not restate their complaint back at them in your own framing.
- Do not explain policy. Nobody reading has ever been persuaded by policy.
- Do not mention anything about the customer the review did not disclose. That is
  the single worst outcome available here.
- Never imply they are lying, even when you believe it.

**If a review looks fake** — no detail, no verifiable transaction, a burst of
similar ones — do not accuse. Flag it for the owner and, if the platform has a
disputes process, say so. Publicly calling a review fake reads as guilt to everyone
except the person who wrote it.

## Case studies

The client agreed to be named, or they did not. If nobody can confirm, it runs
anonymised or not at all. Every number carries where it came from. If the result was
good but partly luck, say what else was going on — a case study that survives
scrutiny is worth more than one that impresses briefly.
