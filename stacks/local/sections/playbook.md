# Local playbook — the hard calls

Read this when details conflict across sources, when sequencing a cleanup, or when
reviews are involved.

## Establishing the canonical details

Before touching anything, get one authoritative version of name, address, phone and
hours — from the owner, in writing. Not from the website, not from the busiest
directory, not from whichever version appears most often.

Frequency is not authority. A wrong phone number copied across nine aggregators is
still wrong, and "most sources agree" is exactly how a business ends up defending
the wrong number to itself.

Where the owner's version and the live listing disagree, ask once, plainly: which is
correct? That single question prevents the entire cleanup being done twice.

## Sequencing a cleanup

1. `local-citation-checker` — inventory first. You cannot fix what you have not
   found, and the count itself tells the owner why they are not ranking.
2. Google Business Profile — fix the primary listing before anything downstream.
   Aggregators re-scrape it, so fixing them first means fixing them again.
3. The major aggregators next, then the long tail. Most small directories pull from
   the big ones on a delay.
4. `local-seo-optimizer` for categories, service area, description and photos, once
   the details underneath are stable.
5. `faq-generator` last — real questions, published once the listing they point at
   is correct.

Expect delay. Directories re-scrape on their own schedule, and nothing here shows
results the same week. Say that up front, because the alternative is being asked in
four days why it has not worked.

## What you cannot do from here

Say so plainly rather than working around it:

- Most directories need account access. Without it you can report what is wrong and
  not fix it.
- Google Business Profile edits can be held for review, sometimes for days. A
  submitted edit is not a live edit, and the report should not imply it is.
- Duplicate listings usually need the owner to file the removal. Prepare it; do not
  claim it is done.

A report that says "found, could not access, here is exactly what to change" is
honest and useful. One that omits the inaccessible ones looks complete and is not.

## Reviews

The local stack watches; Sales replies. If a review needs a response, hand it over
rather than writing one here — one voice replying to customers is the point.

What is in scope: noticing a pattern. Three reviews in a month naming the same
problem is an operational finding the owner needs, and it is worth more than any
listing fix you could make that week.
