# Product Description Writer

Turn a spec sheet into product copy that sells the outcome and ranks for the search.

## Procedure

1. **Read the specs.** Collect product name, materials, dimensions, features, price point, and target customer from the files or notes provided. List what is fact versus what would be a claim needing support. Ask if the target customer or platform (Shopify, Amazon, Etsy) is unclear, since format rules differ.
2. **Research the search.** Search the web for how buyers phrase queries for this product type and what the top-ranking listings emphasize. Pick one primary keyword phrase and 2-3 secondary phrases per product. Note competitor angles to avoid repeating.
3. **Translate features to benefits.** For each spec, write the "so that": waterproof zipper becomes gear stays dry in the rain. Lead every piece of copy with the benefit; the spec is the proof that follows.
4. **Write the description.** Structure per product: title with the primary keyword read naturally (under 70 characters), opening paragraph (2-3 sentences selling the main outcome to the target customer), 4-6 scannable bullets pairing benefit with spec, and a short closing line handling the top objection or use case.
5. **Write the meta description.** 150-160 characters, includes the primary keyword, states the key benefit, and gives a reason to click.
6. **Check the SEO without stuffing.** Primary keyword appears in title, first paragraph, and meta; secondaries appear once each where natural. If a sentence exists only to hold a keyword, cut it.
7. **Deliver.** Write all products to `drafts/product-descriptions-<batch-slug>.md`, one section per product with title, description, bullets, and meta clearly labeled, and report the path with the keyword chosen per product.

## Quality bar

- No unverifiable superlatives ("best," "highest quality") and no invented specs or review counts.
- Copy addresses one specific buyer, not everyone.
- Bullets scan in under ten seconds; no bullet over two lines.
- Each product in a batch reads distinct — shared phrasing across products is rewritten.
