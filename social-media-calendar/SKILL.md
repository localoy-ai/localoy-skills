# Social Media Calendar

Turn a month of themes into a complete, ready-to-post 30-day social calendar.

## Procedure

1. **Collect inputs.** Read the themes, promotions, launch dates, and brand notes the user provides. Check the workspace for a brand voice file or past calendars and reuse their conventions. Ask only if the month, platforms, or posting frequency are missing.
2. **Research the month.** Search the web for holidays, observances, and industry dates in the target month that fit the business. Verify each date before including it; skip anything you cannot confirm.
3. **Set the mix.** Allocate the 30 days across content types: promotional (about 20%), educational, engagement questions, behind-the-scenes, and user-generated or testimonial posts. Anchor promos and launches to their real dates first, then fill around them.
4. **Write platform-specific copy.** For each day, write the actual post copy per platform, not a generic blurb. Respect platform norms: short and punchy for X, conversational with a hook line for Facebook and Instagram, professional for LinkedIn. Include hashtag suggestions and an image or video direction per post.
5. **Add CTAs and links.** Every promotional post gets one clear call to action. Vary CTA wording across the month so the feed does not repeat itself.
6. **Assemble the calendar.** Build a table with columns: date, day of week, platform(s), content type, post copy, hashtags, asset note, CTA/link. Group by week with a short weekly focus line.
7. **Deliver.** Write the calendar to `drafts/social-calendar-<month>.md` and report the path with a two-line summary of the content mix.

## Quality bar

- Every post is publishable as written; no placeholders like "[insert offer]" unless the user has not supplied the detail, and then flag it at the top of the file.
- Dates and observances are verified, never assumed.
- Copy varies in structure day to day; no two consecutive posts open the same way.
- Character limits respected: 280 for X, first-line hook under 125 characters for Instagram.
